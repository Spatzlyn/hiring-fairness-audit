# What Does a Fairness Audit Establish? Evidence from Score-Based Selection in AI Hiring Assessments

Yookyung Youn · Korea University

If a scoring model reveals less about a person's gender or age, does it also produce smaller disparities when those scores are used to select candidates? This project examines how far a finding at one stage of a fairness audit carries over to the decisions that follow.

## Study design

The study uses cognitive-task data from the Human Connectome Project as a controlled analogue for cognitive assessments used in hiring. Participants are ranked by a model score, and the fraction selected is varied while the ranking is held fixed. The resulting selections are experimental outcomes, not observed hiring decisions.

- **From scores to decisions.** Compare how readily demographic attributes can be inferred from scores with group disparities in selection under the same mitigation setting.
- **Across selection rates.** Check whether an improvement at one cutoff persists when a larger or smaller share of the same pool is selected.
- **From groups to individuals.** At a fixed selection rate, measure how often individuals enter or leave the selected group after mitigation.

## What the case study shows

Making demographic attributes harder to infer did not consistently reduce selection disparities. A result reported at a single cutoff also missed disparities visible elsewhere in the selection-rate sweep. Reporting changes in individual selection status alongside group-level measures makes clear how many decisions changed to produce an apparent gain.
